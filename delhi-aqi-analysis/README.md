# Delhi Air Quality Analysis (January 2023)

An intermediate-level Data Science internship project analyzing the provided Delhi pollutant dataset with Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, and Jupyter Notebook.

# Project objective

Explore time variation, distributions, hourly patterns, extreme readings, and relationships among eight recorded pollutant variables. The project follows the internship task's emphasis on research questions, data cleaning, statistical analysis, and visualizations.

# Research questions

1. How do pollutant readings change across the available time period?
2. How are the pollutant values distributed, and how do means, medians, and extremes compare?
3. Do average readings differ by hour of day?
4. Which pollutant variables are most strongly correlated?
5. At what timestamps do the highest readings occur?

# Dataset

File: `delhiaqi.csv`

- **561 hourly records**
- **Observed period:** 1 January 2023 00:00 to 24 January 2023 08:00
- **Columns:** `date`, `co`, `no`, `no2`, `o3`, `so2`, `pm2_5`, `pm10`, `nh3`
- The supplied CSV has no missing values or duplicate rows/timestamps.
- The CSV does not provide measurement units, monitoring-station identifiers/coordinates, or an AQI column.

# Important interpretation note

The task brief asks for an AQI analysis, but the supplied dataset contains pollutant readings rather than an official AQI field. Since the units and source metadata are not documented in the provided file, this project **does not calculate an official AQI or assign health-risk categories**. Values are analyzed in the scale in which they appear in the CSV. Do not compare them with regulatory thresholds without first verifying units and provenance.

The dataset covers less than one month and has no geographical or weather fields. Therefore, it cannot establish seasonal differences, compare neighborhoods/stations, or attribute changes to weather or specific emission sources.

## Methods used

- Data loading, timestamp parsing, sorting, and data-quality checks
- Descriptive statistics: mean, median, standard deviation, minimum, maximum, and skewness
- Hourly time-series visualization
- Histograms and distribution analysis
- Hour-of-day average profiles
- Pearson correlation matrix and strongest pairwise correlations
- Daily averages for selected pollutants
- Peak timestamp identification

## Initial observations

- The dataset contains 561 consecutive hourly observations spanning 1 January through the morning of 24 January 2023.
- The largest recorded `pm2_5` value is **1310.20** at **19 January 2023, 17:00**; `pm10` reaches **1499.27** at the same timestamp. These are dataset-scale values and should be validated against source metadata before environmental interpretation.
- In this sample, `pm2_5` and `pm10` are very strongly positively correlated (Pearson correlation approximately **0.99**). Correlation is not proof of causation.
- The short time window supports exploratory short-term analysis, not conclusions about year-round seasonality or geographical differences.

The notebook contains the full analysis, tables, plots, interpretation notes, and recommendations for extending the project.
